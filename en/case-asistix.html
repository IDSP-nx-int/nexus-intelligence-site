Operational case · Microsoft 365

ASISTIX — Attendance analytics and automation

Real operational case for attendance control, justification requests, approvals, evidence, and dashboards for HR and leadership using Microsoft 365 tools.

Contact

Quick facts

  • Context: internal solution for a public organization
  • Approx. users: 100
  • Monthly requests: approx. 450
  • User area: HR / Leadership / Managers
  • Focus: traceability, reporting, and operational control

1) Context / problem

Attendance and justification control was distributed across emails, files, manual records, and operational queries. This created risk of lost information, inconsistent criteria, low traceability, and difficulty consolidating reports for HR and leadership.

2) Solution

Main deliverables

  • Centralized request intake through Microsoft Forms.
  • Approval flows with Power Automate, Outlook, and request-type rules.
  • Document evidence stored and linked through SharePoint.
  • Structured logs in Excel Online as an operational source for query and reporting.
  • Power BI dashboards for absences, lateness, justifications, and incidents.

Operational flow

  • Employee submits a request with required fields.
  • Power Automate applies validations and sends approval.
  • Manager approves or rejects.
  • Outcome is notified and registered in the log.
  • Power BI consumes the information for operational and executive reporting.

The solution helped turn a manual process into a more structured, traceable, and measurable workflow.

3) Analytical model and architecture

Data model

  • Employee-day labor calendar as the analytical backbone.
  • Biometric records as the source of clock-in/clock-out events.
  • Catalogs for employees, schedules, holidays, and justification types.
  • Justification logs from forms and operational records.

Automation and reporting

  • Power Automate for validations, approvals, notifications, and result logging.
  • SharePoint for evidence and related files.
  • Excel Online as an operational log and structured source.
  • Power BI for models, DAX measures, dashboards, and monthly analysis.

4) Key features

  • Incident classification: normal, late, absent, justified, or pending.
  • Request windows: control of allowed periods and rules by justification type.
  • Traceability: requester, approver, date, outcome, evidence, and status.
  • Operational reporting: follow-up by employee, area, month, incident type, and status.
  • Employee-day model: analyzes what should have happened and what actually happened.

5) Metrics and scope

Approximate scale

  • Users: approx. 100
  • Monthly requests: approx. 450
  • Sources: Forms, Excel Online, SharePoint, SQL/MySQL, biometric system.
  • Reports: operational and executive Power BI reporting.

Indicators worked on

  • Absences and lateness.
  • Approved, rejected, or pending justifications.
  • Incidents by area, employee, and period.
  • Employees with recurring incidents.
  • Monthly follow-up for HR and leadership.

7) Future improvements

  • Migrate operational logs from Excel Online to Dataverse for stronger governance.
  • Add automatic reminders and escalation for pending approvals.
  • Formalize Dev/Test/Prod environments for controlled packaging and deployment.
  • Expand dashboards with trend, recurrence, and response-time metrics.
