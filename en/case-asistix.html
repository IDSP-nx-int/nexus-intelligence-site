Attendance Analytics and Employee Justification Automation

An implemented Human Resources case for attendance control, employee justification requests, approvals, evidence, operational traceability, and Power BI reporting using Microsoft 365 tools. This case is documented in the portfolio under the ASISTIX name.

View Portfolio LinkedIn

Quick facts

  • Context: Internal HR solution for a public-sector organization
  • Approx. users: 100
  • Monthly requests: Approximately 450
  • User groups: HR, managers, employees, and leadership
  • Focus: Traceability, reporting, and operational control

1) Context and problem

Attendance and employee justification processes were distributed across emails, files, manual records, and repeated operational questions. This created risks of lost information, inconsistent criteria, limited traceability, and difficulty consolidating reports for Human Resources and leadership.

2) Implemented solution

Main components

  • Standardized request intake through Microsoft Forms.
  • Approval workflows with Power Automate, Outlook, and request-type rules.
  • Document evidence stored and linked through SharePoint.
  • Structured operational logs in Excel Online.
  • Power BI dashboards for absences, late arrivals, justifications, and attendance incidents.

Operational flow

  • An employee submits a request with the required information.
  • Power Automate applies validations and sends the request for approval.
  • The corresponding manager approves or rejects it.
  • The result is communicated and registered in the operational log.
  • Power BI uses the consolidated information for operational and management reporting.

The solution converted a manual process into a more structured, traceable, and measurable workflow.

3) Analytical model and architecture

Data model

  • Employee-day calendar as the analytical foundation.
  • Biometric records as the source of clock-in and clock-out events.
  • Catalogs for employees, schedules, holidays, and justification types.
  • Justification logs from forms and operational records.

Automation and reporting

  • Power Automate for validations, approvals, notifications, and result logging.
  • SharePoint for evidence and supporting files.
  • Excel Online as a structured operational source.
  • Power BI for models, DAX measures, dashboards, and monthly analysis.

4) Key features

  • Attendance classification: normal, late, absent, justified, or pending.
  • Request windows: control of permitted periods and rules by request type.
  • Traceability: requester, approver, date, result, evidence, and status.
  • Operational reporting: analysis by employee, area, month, incident type, and status.
  • Employee-day analysis: comparison of expected and actual attendance behavior.

5) Metrics and scope

Approximate scale

  • Users: Approximately 100
  • Monthly requests: Approximately 450
  • Sources: Forms, Excel Online, SharePoint, SQL/MySQL, and biometric data
  • Reports: Operational and management Power BI reporting

Indicators analyzed

  • Absences and late arrivals.
  • Approved, rejected, and pending requests.
  • Attendance incidents by area, employee, and period.
  • Employees with recurring attendance incidents.
  • Monthly follow-up for Human Resources and leadership.

6) Potential next improvements

  • Migrate operational logs from Excel Online to Dataverse.
  • Add automated reminders and escalation for pending approvals.
  • Formalize Dev/Test/Prod environments for controlled deployment.
  • Expand dashboards with trends, recurrence, and response-time indicators.
